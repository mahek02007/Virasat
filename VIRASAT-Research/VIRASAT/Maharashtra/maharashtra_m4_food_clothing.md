# Maharashtra Cultural Heritage Knowledge Base
## Module 4: Food & Cuisine and Clothing & Textiles

### Part 10: Food & Cuisine
Maharashtra's cuisine is broadly divided into distinct regional styles, heavily influenced by geography, climate, and historical communities.

#### Regional Cuisines
1. **Konkan (Coastal)**
   * **Focus:** Seafood, coconut, rice, kokum.
   * **Key Dishes:** Malvani fish curries (using fiery Malvani masala), Bombil (Bombay duck) fry, Kombdi Wade (chicken curry with multi-flour puris).
   * **Drinks:** Sol Kadhi (coconut milk and kokum).
2. **Desh (Western Maharashtra)**
   * **Focus:** Peanuts, jaggery, jowar, bajra.
   * **Key Dishes:** Misal Pav (spicy sprout curry), Pithla Bhakri, Zunka, Bharli Vangi (stuffed eggplants).
   * **Kolhapur Specialty:** Tambda Rassa (red spicy mutton broth), Pandhra Rassa (white broth), Kolhapuri Misal.
3. **Vidarbha (Eastern Maharashtra)**
   * **Focus:** Spicy, peanut and besan-heavy.
   * **Key Dishes:** Saoji cuisine (extremely spicy non-veg dishes), Patodi Rassa (besan squares in spicy gravy).
4. **Khandesh (Northwestern Maharashtra)**
   * **Focus:** Peanuts, dry coconut, very spicy.
   * **Key Dishes:** Khandeshi Rassa, Baingan Bharta (Khandeshi style), Shev Bhaji.
5. **Marathwada**
   * **Focus:** Millets, local pulses, slightly less spicy than Vidarbha.
   * **Key Dishes:** Amti (sour dal), Shengdana Chutney.

#### Important Dishes Database
- **Puran Poli**
  - *Type:* Sweet flatbread.
  - *Ingredients:* Chana dal, jaggery/sugar, cardamom, wheat flour.
  - *Significance:* Festival food, especially Holi and Gudi Padwa.
- **Modak (Ukdiche Modak)**
  - *Type:* Sweet dumpling (Steamed).
  - *Ingredients:* Rice flour shell, fresh coconut and jaggery filling.
  - *Significance:* Central to Ganesh Chaturthi festival.
- **Misal Pav**
  - *Type:* Spicy street food/breakfast.
  - *Variations:* Puneri Misal (milder, sweeter), Kolhapuri (very spicy), Nashik Misal.
- **Vada Pav**
  - *Type:* Street food.
  - *History:* Originated in 1960s/70s in Mumbai as a quick, cheap meal for mill workers.
- **Thalipeeth**
  - *Type:* Savory multi-flour pancake (Bhajanee).
  - *Ingredients:* Roasted grains, legumes, spices.
- **Shrikhand & Aamras**
  - *Type:* Sweet desserts (strained yogurt; mango pulp).
- **Tribal Foods**
  - *Ingredients:* Bamboo shoots, local tubers, millets (Nachni), dried fish, Mahua flowers.

### Part 11: Clothing & Textiles
Maharashtra has a rich textile tradition with several items holding Geographical Indication (GI) tags.

#### Women's Clothing
- **Nauvari Saree (Kashta)**
  - *History:* 9-yard saree worn in a trouser-like style (kashta). Historically allowed Maratha women to ride horses and fight in battles.
  - *Current Status:* Worn during festivals, weddings, and cultural events.
- **Paithani Saree**
  - *History:* Dates back to Satavahana dynasty. Woven in Paithan and Yeola.
  - *Technique:* Tapestry weaving technique, pure silk, zari (gold/silver threads).
  - *Motifs:* Peacocks (mor bangadi), lotuses, parrots.
  - *GI Tag:* Yes.
- **Lugade**
  - *Usage:* Everyday cotton saree, often 5 or 8 yards.

#### Men's Clothing
- **Dhotar:** Traditional unstitched garment tied around the waist and legs.
- **Pheta (Turban):**
  - *Types:* Puneri pheta (symbol of pride, often worn by dignitaries), Kolhapuri pheta, Mawali pheta.
- **Gandhi Topi:** White cap made of khadi, popularized during the freedom struggle.

#### Footwear & Jewelry
- **Kolhapuri Chappal**
  - *History & Making:* Handcrafted leather footwear, dyed with vegetable dyes. Known for intricate braiding.
  - *GI Tag:* Shared between Maharashtra and Karnataka.
- **Traditional Jewelry**
  - *Thushi:* Choker-style necklace.
  - *Kolhapuri Saaj:* Traditional necklace with 21 different leaves/pendants.
  - *Nath:* Pearl nose ring, worn on special occasions.

#### Other Textiles
- **Himroo (Aurangabad):** Blend of cotton and silk, known for intricate floral designs, linked to the Tughlaq era.
- **Khun Fabric:** Lightweight cotton-silk blend, typically used for blouses, originating from Guledgudda/Ilkal region borders.
- **Karvath Kati:** Woven in Vidarbha (Bhandara/Nagpur), known for saw-edged borders.
