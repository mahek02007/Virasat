## PART 1: Maharashtra Overview

3. **Capital:** Mumbai (Summer), Nagpur (Winter)

13. **Economy:** Largest state economy in India (GDP)

15. **UNESCO World Heritage Sites:** Ajanta Caves, Ellora Caves, Elephanta Caves, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Victorian Gothic & Art Deco Ensembles of Mumbai

### Maharashtra in 100 Words
Maharashtra, located in the western and central peninsular region of India, is the nation's economic powerhouse and third-largest state by area. Formed on May 1, 1960, its capital is Mumbai. Geographically diverse, it features the lush Sahyadri mountains, the expansive Deccan plateau, and a 720-km Konkan coastline. Steeped in history, it is the land of the great Maratha warrior Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and a rich tradition of saint-poets like Dnyaneshwar and Tukaram. Maharashtra boasts incredible cultural heritage, vibrant festivals like Ganesh Chaturthi, ancient rock-cut caves, and a dynamic synthesis of tradition and modernity. [HIGH]

### Maharashtra in 500 Words
Maharashtra is one of India's most industrially advanced and economically significant states, seamlessly blending a rich historical legacy with rapid modern development. Located in the western part of the country, it was formed on May 1, 1960, following the Samyukta Maharashtra movement which demanded a separate Marathi-speaking state. 

**Geography and Climate:** The state is geographically dominated by the Deccan Plateau, separated from the Konkan coastline by the Western Ghats (Sahyadris). The Godavari and Krishna rivers, originating in the Western Ghats, flow eastwards across the plateau, making it fertile. The climate is tropical monsoon, with heavy rainfall in the Konkan and Sahyadris, while parts of Marathwada and Vidarbha fall in the rain-shadow region, making them prone to droughts.

**History and Heritage:** Maharashtra’s history is ancient and diverse. From the early Satavahana and Vakataka dynasties (who contributed to the magnificent Ajanta and Ellora caves) to the Rashtrakutas and Yadavas, the region has been a crucible of culture. The 17th century saw the rise of the Maratha Empire under the visionary leadership of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, whose establishment of "Hindavi Swarajya" reshaped Indian history. The subsequent Peshwa era expanded Maratha influence across the subcontinent. [HISTORICAL FACT]

**Economy:** Mumbai, the capital, is the financial capital of India, housing the Bombay Stock Exchange, RBI, and the prolific Bollywood film industry. Pune is an IT and educational hub (often called the "Oxford of the East"), while Nashik is known for agriculture and wine production. Nagpur, the winter capital, is centrally located and famous for its oranges.

**Culture and Society:** Maharashtra's culture is rooted in the Bhakti movement, led by saints like Namdev, Eknath, Tukaram, and Ramdas, who promoted social equality. Marathi, the official language, has a rich literary tradition. The state celebrates festivals with grand fervor, most notably Ganesh Chaturthi and Gudi Padwa. The cuisine is diverse, from the fiery curries of Vidarbha and Kolhapur to the coastal seafood of Konkan. Folk arts like Lavani dance and Powada (ballads) [FOLK TRADITION] keep the martial and cultural history alive. [HIGH]


## PART 2: Administrative Structure

Maharashtra is divided into 6 administrative divisions and 36 districts. [HIGH]

### 1. Konkan Division
**Districts:** Mumbai City, Mumbai Suburban, Thane, Palghar, Raigad, Ratnagiri, Sindhudurg
*   **Mumbai City & Suburban:** Capital, financial hub, diverse immigrant communities, Koli fisherfolk (original inhabitants). [HIGH]
*   **Thane & Palghar:** Mix of urban sprawl and tribal (Warli) communities. Warli painting [FOLK TRADITION].
*   **Raigad:** Historical capital of Shivaji Maharaj (Raigad Fort). Coastal forts like Murud Janjira.
*   **Ratnagiri & Sindhudurg:** Alphonso mangoes, Malvani cuisine, pristine beaches, Dashavatar folk theatre [FOLK TRADITION]. Sindhudurg fort. 

### 2. Pune Division
**Districts:** Pune, Satara, Sangli, Kolhapur, Solapur
*   **Pune:** Educational/IT hub, Peshwa history (Shaniwar Wada), cultural capital.
*   **Satara & Sangli:** Sugarcane belt, turmeric city (Sangli), Kaas Plateau (UNESCO natural site).
*   **Kolhapur:** Mahalaxmi Temple [RELIGIOUS BELIEF], Kolhapuri chappals, spicy cuisine (Tambda/Pandhra rassa), Kushti (wrestling).
*   **Solapur:** Textile hub (Solapuri chaddar), Siddheshwar Temple, border culture with Karnataka.

### 3. Nashik Division
**Districts:** Nashik, Ahmednagar, Dhule, Nandurbar, Jalgaon
*   **Nashik:** Kumbh Mela site (Trimbakeshwar), wine capital, agriculture (grapes, onions).
*   **Ahmednagar:** Largest district, Shirdi Sai Baba [RELIGIOUS BELIEF], Ralegan Siddhi (model village).
*   **Jalgaon & Dhule:** Banana capital, gold markets, Khandeshi culture.
*   **Nandurbar:** Primarily tribal district, bordering Gujarat and MP.

### 4. Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) Division (Marathwada)
**Districts:** Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Jalna, Parbhani, Hingoli, Nanded, Beed, Latur, Dharashiv (Osmanabad)
*   **Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar:** Tourism capital, Ajanta & Ellora caves, Daulatabad Fort, Bibi Ka Maqbara.
*   **Nanded:** Takht Sachkhand Sri Hazur Sahib (Sikh pilgrimage) [RELIGIOUS BELIEF].
*   **Latur & Beed:** Agricultural hub, drought-prone, historical temples.

### 5. Amravati Division (Western Vidarbha)
**Districts:** Amravati, Akola, Washim, Buldhana, Yavatmal
*   **Amravati:** Melghat Tiger Reserve, Rukmini temple [RELIGIOUS BELIEF].
*   **Buldhana:** Lonar Crater Lake (created by meteorite impact).
*   **Yavatmal & Akola:** Cotton belt ("White Gold").

### 6. Nagpur Division (Eastern Vidarbha)
**Districts:** Nagpur, Wardha, Bhandara, Gondia, Chandrapur, Gadchiroli
*   **Nagpur:** Winter capital, Orange City, Deekshabhoomi (where Dr. B.R. Ambedkar embraced Buddhism) [HISTORICAL FACT].
*   **Wardha:** Sevagram (Mahatma Gandhi's ashram).
*   **Chandrapur & Gondia:** Tadoba Andhari Tiger Reserve, coal mining, dense forests.
*   **Gadchiroli:** Heavily forested, tribal communities (Madia Gond), naxalite affected.

## PART 4: History of Maharashtra

### Timeline
*   **c. 230 BCE - 225 CE:** Satavahana Dynasty (Pratishthana/Paithan as capital).
*   **c. 250 - 500 CE:** Vakataka Dynasty (Patrons of Ajanta Caves).
*   **6th - 12th Century:** Chalukyas and Rashtrakutas (Ellora Caves).
*   **12th - 14th Century:** Yadavas of Devagiri (Golden age of Marathi language).
*   **13th - 16th Century:** Islamic Sultanates (Bahmani, Nizam Shahi, Adil Shahi).
*   **1630 - 1680:** Life of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj (Founding of Maratha Empire). [HISTORICAL FACT]
*   **18th Century:** Peshwa Era (Expansion of Maratha power across India).
*   **1818:** Third Anglo-Maratha War (British annex Maratha territories, form Bombay Presidency).
*   **19th - 20th Century:** Social Reform Movement (Mahatma Phule, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Shahu Maharaj) & Freedom Struggle (Lokmanya Tilak).
*   **May 1, 1960:** Formation of the modern State of Maharashtra.

### Detailed Periods
*   **Ancient:** The Satavahanas were the first major indigenous dynasty, fostering trade and patronizing Buddhism (rock-cut caves at Karla, Bhaja). The Vakatakas succeeded them, famously creating the later Buddhist caves at Ajanta.
*   **Medieval:** The Yadavas of Devagiri patronized Marathi. Saint Dnyaneshwar wrote the *Dnyaneshwari* during this period. After the Khilji invasion, the region was ruled by the Bahmani Sultanate, which later split into Deccan Sultanates.
*   **Maratha Period:** Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj carved out *Swarajya* using guerrilla warfare (*ganimi kava*) from the Sahyadri forts. His administration featured the *Ashta Pradhan* (council of 8 ministers). Later, the Peshwas (Prime Ministers) based in Pune expanded the empire from Attock to Cuttack. [SOURCE DISAGREEMENT: While widely celebrated as a Hindu kingdom, modern scholars emphasize the Maratha state's secular administrative policies and inclusion of Muslim commanders in the navy and artillery].
*   **Colonial & Modern:** Maharashtra was the epicenter of the Indian social reform movement, fighting caste discrimination and championing women's education. Mumbai became a bustling mill town and the nerve center of the Indian National Congress. After independence, the Samyukta Maharashtra Samiti successfully fought for a Marathi linguistic state, culminating in the 1960 formation. [HIGH]
